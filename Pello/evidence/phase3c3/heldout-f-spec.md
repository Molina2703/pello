# AssemblyCount: cooperative meeting credentials and voting

## Purpose and boundaries
A housing cooperative needs an auditable record of meeting eligibility, proxy authority and voting outcomes. The pilot covers one meeting, up to 2,000 memberships, 20 ordinary motions and one single-seat director election. Each eligible membership has one vote per contest, regardless of how many people own it. The approved membership roll explicitly states eligibility; the service does not infer it from arrears or ownership documents. The service does not collect dues, resolve ownership disputes, conduct campaigning, send member messages or change bylaws. Synthetic rolls, proxy grants and ballots are available; external credentials, accounts and operation authorizations are absent.

## Eligibility and proxy credentials
A secretary can preview and accept a membership roll containing meeting_id, membership_id, eligible and source_reference. Missing fields, repeated membership identifiers or invalid eligibility values reject the entire roll with row-level reasons and no changes. Accepting a replacement creates a new roll version and retains the previous one. The meeting chair must freeze a roll version before counting; that version fixes the eligible-membership denominator. Later corrections require a reasoned amendment and a new freeze, making affected draft results stale without overwriting certified history.

A proxy grant names the represented membership, its holder membership, valid_from, valid_until and evidence reference. Both memberships must be eligible on the frozen roll and must differ. Use UTC timestamps and half-open validity intervals; end must be later than start. A membership cannot have overlapping grants to different holders. A holder may represent at most two other memberships at any instant; its own vote does not consume that limit. Proxy authority cannot be delegated onward. A revocation shortens the grant's validity and preserves actor, time and reason; it does not invalidate a ballot validly cast before the revocation took effect. Credential validation can be planned locally before a voting-provider connection exists.

## Ballot evidence and selection
Voting opens at 14:00 UTC and closes at 16:00 UTC on the configured meeting date. Opening is inclusive and closing exclusive. A secretary can preview a local ballot file containing ballot_id, contest_id, represented_membership_id, casting_membership_id, cast_at and choice. Ordinary choices are YES, NO or ABSTAIN; director choices are one declared candidate identifier or ABSTAIN. Reject a whole file for malformed rows, unknown contests or undeclared choices, with row-level reasons and no changes. An identical ballot identifier and content is a no-op; changed content under that identifier is rejected. Preserve every imported ballot and its validation explanation.

Ballots outside the voting window or from ineligible memberships are retained as invalid evidence and never counted. A direct ballot has matching represented and casting identifiers. A proxy ballot requires the holder's grant to cover cast_at. For each membership and contest, the latest valid direct ballot takes precedence over every proxy ballot, even a later proxy ballot. If there is no valid direct ballot, use the latest valid proxy ballot. Conflicting choices with the same latest timestamp leave that membership's contest vote unresolved; do not break the tie by import order or ballot identifier. An unresolved selected vote blocks certification of that contest. Ballot parsing, validation and selection can be planned with synthetic files independently of external access.

## Ordinary motions
For each motion, quorum requires selected valid ballots from at least 60% of eligible memberships on the frozen roll, rounded upward to a whole membership. Count represented memberships, not people attending or proxy holders. ABSTAIN counts toward quorum but not the decision denominator. With quorum, a motion passes only when YES is strictly greater than NO; a tie or zero YES and zero NO does not pass. Without quorum, report NO QUORUM rather than a failed motion. A nonvoting eligible membership remains in the quorum denominator. Results must show the frozen-roll version, eligible count, quorum requirement, represented count and YES, NO and ABSTAIN totals.

## Director-election contradiction
The signed election rules state “proxy votes are permitted in the director election.” The current adopted meeting resolution states “proxy votes are prohibited in the director election.” Both are mandatory and no precedence ruling exists. The cooperative's election officer must resolve this contradiction. Retain structurally valid director proxy ballots as unresolved evidence; do not silently count or discard them. Director certification is blocked until resolution. Ordinary-motion credentials, selection and counting must proceed meanwhile. Once proxy policy is resolved, the director winner is the candidate with the most selected valid votes, excluding ABSTAIN; equal highest totals or no candidate votes produce NO WINNER and require a separately convened election, not an automatic draw. Users disagree whether results should initially list motions by agenda order or title; either presentation is acceptable and must not change outcomes.

## Review, privacy and publication
An election officer can prepare a versioned result package and certify an unblocked contest only against the current frozen roll, credentials and ballot evidence. Certification must reject stale calculations. Changes affecting a certified result require a separately reviewed amendment linked to the original; never overwrite the certified package. The review overview must show credential violations, invalid ballots, unresolved selections, quorum outcomes, stale calculations and publication status with supporting references. Public results contain aggregate totals only. Viewers can read aggregates; secretaries maintain rolls, credentials and ballot intake; election officers inspect individual evidence, resolve director policy and certify results; only the meeting chair freezes rolls and authorizes publication. Enforce permissions beyond screen visibility, including restrictions on access to individual choices. Retained audit evidence must support authorized review without exposing individual choices publicly.

The external voting provider is expected to supply ballot exports and publish certified results to the member portal. Its endpoint, authentication, meeting permissions, ballot authenticity evidence, authoritative timestamp semantics, publication schema and duplicate-publication behavior are unknown. These block trusted live exchange, not local synthetic validation or ordinary counting. Before a publication attempt, the meeting chair must separately authorize the exact certified package version, meeting and contest set. This brief grants no execution permission. Changed results invalidate that authorization; certification alone is not publication permission. Recheck currency and authorization immediately before sending. Preserve rejected, accepted for processing, published and unknown outcomes; acceptance is not publication. A timeout is unknown and requires documented outcome resolution before another attempt, without automatic retry. Link outcomes to the attempted package and authorization.

## Acceptance and delivery expectations
With 101 eligible memberships, quorum is 61. Totals YES=30, NO=30 and ABSTAIN=1 meet quorum but do not pass; YES=31, NO=29 and ABSTAIN=1 pass. Sixty represented memberships produce NO QUORUM. One holder voting for itself and two represented memberships contributes three membership votes, not one. A valid direct YES at 14:10 defeats a valid proxy NO at 15:00 for the same membership and motion. A ballot at 16:00 is invalid. Two different latest direct choices both timestamped 15:30 remain unresolved. No director winner is asserted before proxy-policy resolution.

Rolls, grants, ballots, explanations, certified versions and authorization history must survive restart with identical selected votes and totals. Local storage, preview layout and initial result ordering may use explicitly labeled reversible assumptions; none is customer approval. Describe verification as proposed work; no implementation, executed tests or live-provider evidence is supplied.
