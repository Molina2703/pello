# ParcelProof: survey parcel checks and cadastral submission review

## Purpose and limits
A small land-surveying firm needs to turn approved survey points into checked parcel schedules and reviewed cadastral submissions. The pilot covers 15 projects, each with at most 30 parcels and 200 boundary vertices per parcel. Coordinates are integer millimetres in a named project reference system. The service does not operate surveying instruments, transform coordinate systems, determine ownership, design buildings, value land or manage easements. Synthetic point files and parcel outlines are available; external credentials, accounts and submission authorizations are absent.

## Survey points
A survey technician can preview a point file containing project_id, point_id, easting_mm, northing_mm and evidence_reference. Missing fields, noninteger coordinates or duplicate point identifiers reject the entire file with row-level reasons and no changes. Point identifiers are unique within a project. Reimporting identical point content is a no-op; changed coordinates require a reasoned revision preserving the original values, actor and time. A licensed surveyor approves point revisions for use in parcel checks. Imported but unapproved points remain visible and cannot support an approved parcel schedule.

## Parcel geometry and measurements
A technician records each parcel's identifier and ordered boundary point identifiers. All points must belong to the same project and reference system. An outline must repeat its first point at the end, contain at least three distinct vertices and have no consecutive coincident coordinates. Reject an open outline, a zero-length edge, a self-crossing boundary or a boundary touching itself anywhere except its closing endpoint. Do not reorder vertices, snap coordinates or repair geometry automatically. Report the reason a parcel cannot be measured.

For a valid outline, calculate area from its coordinates using the shoelace rule, taking the absolute result so clockwise and counterclockwise outlines give the same area. Retain the unrounded area for comparisons; display square metres to two decimal places with exact halves rounded upward. The schedule must also show each edge's endpoint identifiers and length in metres to three decimal places. Display rounding must not change geometry validation. Point validation and geometry checks can be planned separately with synthetic examples before external cadastral access exists.

The technician can declare two parcels adjoining and name their shared boundary chains. Their shared geometry must coincide exactly in reverse traversal; extra collinear intermediate vertices are acceptable. Any positive-area overlap between project parcels is an error, while a shared edge is allowed. A gap between declared adjoining chains is an error even if small. Do not average the boundaries or invent a tolerance. Unrelated parcels need not touch.

## Boundary dispute and review
For parcel P-7, the signed deed sketch states “the western boundary follows points A–B,” while the client's signed instruction states “the western boundary follows points C–D.” The two lines differ, both documents are described as mandatory, and no precedence decision exists. The client's conveyancer must supply an authoritative resolution. Preserve both claims without selecting one. P-7's approved schedule and submission remain blocked; other parcels and project point review can proceed. Candidate P-7 outlines may be checked locally only when clearly labeled disputed and unapproved.

A licensed surveyor can approve a versioned parcel schedule only when its points are approved, geometry and adjoining checks pass, and relevant disputes are resolved. Changes to supporting points, outlines or adjoining declarations make affected approvals stale. Previously submitted schedules remain immutable; corrections require a newly reviewed amendment linked to the original submission. The overview must expose unapproved points, geometry errors, adjoining mismatches, disputes, stale approvals and submission outcomes. Users disagree whether it should initially group parcels by client reference or parcel identifier; either is acceptable and must not change checks.

## External submission and authority
The cadastral agency is expected to accept parcel schedules and amendments and return lodgement outcomes. Its endpoint, authentication, firm permissions, required reference-system identification, coordinate precision, package schema and duplicate-lodgement behavior are unknown. These block live submission, not local checks. Do not assume that agency receipt establishes ownership or legal registration.

Before any attempt, the firm's principal surveyor must separately authorize the exact approved package version, project, parcel set and destination. This brief grants no execution permission. Schedule approval is not submission authorization. Changed package or destination details invalidate authorization. Immediately before sending, reject stale or unauthorized packages rather than silently rebuilding them. Preserve rejected, received, registered and unknown outcomes; registered status requires the agency's explicit registration evidence. A timeout is unknown and requires documented outcome resolution before another attempt, without automatic retry. Link outcomes to the attempted package and authorization.

Technicians import points and prepare outlines and adjoining declarations; licensed surveyors approve points and schedules; only the principal surveyor authorizes submission. Viewers can read schedules. Enforce permissions beyond screen visibility. The service records the conveyancer's dispute resolution without deciding ownership itself.

## Acceptance and delivery expectations
A closed rectangle 10,000 mm by 20,000 mm has area 200.00 m², regardless of traversal direction. A 10,001 mm by 5,000 mm rectangle has exact area 50.005 m² and displays 50.01 m². Omitting the repeated starting point rejects the outline. A crossing bow-tie outline fails rather than receiving an area. Two squares sharing an edge may pass; a one-millimetre gap between their declared adjoining chains fails. An approved schedule becomes stale after a supporting point revision. P-7 is not approved before authoritative resolution.

Evidence, revisions, approvals and submission history must survive restart with unchanged check results. Local storage, preview layout and initial grouping may use explicitly labeled reversible assumptions; these are not customer approvals. Describe verification as proposed work: no implementation, executed tests or live-agency evidence is supplied.
