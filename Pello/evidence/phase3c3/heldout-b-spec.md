# ArchiveLedger: municipal digital-records custody and storage planning

## Purpose and operating boundary
A municipal archive needs to reconcile deposited digital records, forecast storage commitments and prepare lawful disposition reviews. The pilot covers one repository, three depositing departments and up to 50,000 objects. An object is one immutable file version with a permanent object identifier; a replacement file is a new object. Sizes are positive integer bytes; 1 GiB means 1,073,741,824 bytes. Dates use the municipal calendar in America/Bogota. The service does not alter source files, deduplicate content, search document contents, decide legal disputes or operate backup restoration. Synthetic manifests and deposits are available. No external credentials, service account or execution authorizations are supplied.

## Custody inventory and reconciliation
An archivist can preview and accept a CSV manifest containing snapshot_id, captured_at, object_id, department, record_class, closed_date and size_bytes. Each manifest declares a complete repository snapshot. Reject the entire import, with row-level reasons and no changes, if identifiers repeat, a department is unknown, a size is invalid or a required value is absent. Accept only snapshots captured later than the latest accepted snapshot. Reimporting the same snapshot identifier with identical content is a no-op; changed content under that identifier is rejected. An existing object's department, class, closed date and size cannot change in a later snapshot; corrections require a separately recorded, reasoned metadata amendment, while replacing file content requires a new object identifier.

An object missing from a new snapshot remains in the custody history as missing and creates an exception; it is not automatically classified as lawfully destroyed. A previously missing object that reappears becomes present again and closes its missing exception. New identifiers become present objects. Preserve snapshot provenance and the history of amendments and exceptions. Users need department totals for present bytes, missing-object counts, committed deposits and unresolved exceptions, with links to supporting snapshot or deposit records.

## Deposit commitments and capacity
The repository has a hard capacity of 10,000 GiB and a planning ceiling of 9,500 GiB. A deposit proposal has a department, expected arrival date, positive byte quantity and unique reference. Draft proposals consume no capacity. A records manager can commit or cancel a proposal. Commitment is allowed only if current present bytes plus all outstanding committed deposit bytes, including the proposal, do not exceed 9,500 GiB. Equality is allowed. The hard capacity is never an authorization to exceed the planning ceiling. Competing commitments must not spend the same headroom. Show a dated forecast starting from present bytes and adding outstanding commitments in arrival-date order; overdue commitments remain outstanding.

Receiving a deposit requires an accepted snapshot showing its newly arrived object identifiers and an explicit link to its proposal. Receiving atomically replaces the proposal's reserved bytes with the actual linked object bytes already counted by that snapshot; never count them twice. Every linked object must belong to the proposal's department and may satisfy only one proposal. An actual deposit larger than its commitment is still recorded, with a capacity exception if the resulting total exceeds the ceiling; do not hide received records to preserve a forecast. Cancelling a received deposit cannot remove its objects. Expected destruction never creates headroom. Only an accepted later snapshot showing objects absent reduces present bytes, and unexplained absence still raises a custody exception.

## Retention, holds and a disputed class
For ordinary correspondence, retain each object through 31 December of the third calendar year after its closed_date year. It first becomes eligible for disposition on the following 1 January. Thus an ordinary object closed on 15 June 2022 first becomes eligible on 1 January 2026. Eligibility is evaluated as of a displayed review date, not the date on which a screen was last opened. An authorized records manager can place and release a legal hold, recording actor, time, reason and affected object identifiers. Any active hold prevents disposition regardless of age; releasing a hold restores the normal retention test and does not itself authorize destruction.

For procurement records, the approved retention schedule says “retain through the end of the sixth calendar year after closure,” while the municipality's legal bulletin says “retain through the end of the ninth calendar year after closure.” Both are mandatory documents and no precedence ruling exists. The municipal legal officer must resolve this contradiction. Procurement disposition must remain blocked meanwhile; ordinary correspondence review, holds, inventory reconciliation and capacity planning must proceed. Users also disagree whether review lists should initially sort by department or eligibility date. Either initial sort is acceptable and must not change eligibility or authorization.

## Disposition review and external effect
A reviewer can prepare a versioned candidate list containing only present, eligible, unheld objects and inspect each retention calculation. Preparation does not reserve space or authorize destruction. Submission requires the municipal legal officer's separate authorization of the exact candidate-list version, object identifiers and total bytes. This brief grants no execution permission. Any change to membership or supporting metadata invalidates that authorization. Recheck presence, retention and holds immediately before submission; a newly applied hold or changed supporting metadata blocks the stale list rather than silently removing objects and submitting a different list. Viewers can read; archivists can import and amend inventory metadata; records managers can manage deposit commitments and legal holds; reviewers can prepare lists; only the municipal legal officer can authorize external destruction requests. Enforce these permissions at the operation boundary, beyond screen visibility.

The external preservation provider is expected to supply repository snapshots and accept destruction requests. Its endpoint, authentication, tenant permissions, snapshot completeness guarantee, deletion-request schema, acknowledgement semantics and duplicate-request handling are unknown. These are blockers for live exchange, not for local manifest reconciliation or forecasting. Preserve distinct request outcomes: rejected, accepted for processing, confirmed completed and unknown. Acceptance alone neither proves deletion nor releases capacity. A timeout is unknown; another attempt requires documented outcome resolution, with no automatic retry. Even confirmed completion releases capacity only when a later accepted snapshot shows absence. Absence matching that confirmed request closes the custody exception as explained destruction while retaining its history.

## Acceptance and delivery expectations
With 9,000 GiB present and a 400 GiB outstanding commitment, a new 100 GiB commitment succeeds; a new 101 GiB commitment against the original state fails. If the 400 GiB deposit arrives as 430 GiB, present becomes 9,430 GiB and that commitment becomes zero, rather than reporting 9,830 GiB. An eligible ordinary object under an active hold cannot enter a candidate list. A deletion request accepted for processing leaves present bytes unchanged. A later snapshot omitting an object without confirmed destruction reduces present bytes but creates a missing-object exception.

Business records and authorization history must survive restart with consistent capacity and custody results. Local storage choice, CSV preview layout and initial list sorting may use explicitly labeled reversible assumptions; these are not customer approvals. Inventory reconciliation and deposit forecasting are independently plannable with synthetic data before provider access or procurement-policy resolution. Ordinary retention and hold review can also proceed locally. Verification described in a plan is proposed work: no implementation, executed tests, live integration checks or independent isolation evidence are supplied.
