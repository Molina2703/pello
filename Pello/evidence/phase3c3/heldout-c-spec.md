# KilnBook: timber drying, recovery and export-treatment review

## Operating purpose and limits
A timber processor needs to account for received timber packs, reserve kiln capacity and reconcile recovered boards before releasing stock. The pilot covers one yard, two kilns named K1 and K2, and at most 500 active packs. Each kiln holds 12 standard packs. Every pack has an immutable identifier, supplier reference, species, thickness band and positive integer board count. Packs occupy one slot regardless of board count. The service does not control kiln equipment, prescribe drying chemistry, price timber, arrange transport or issue statutory certificates itself. Synthetic receipts and treatment observations are available; external credentials, accounts and execution authorizations are absent.

## Intake and stock traceability
A yard clerk can preview and accept a receipt containing a unique receipt reference and its pack records. Unknown species or thickness bands, repeated pack identifiers, missing supplier references and invalid counts reject the whole receipt with row-level explanations and no stock changes. An identical receipt reference and content is a no-op; changed content under the same reference is rejected. Packs cannot be reused under another receipt. Corrections require an attributable amendment preserving the original values and reason. A correction to a pack already assigned to an approved batch must block that batch from starting until reviewed and approved again.

Stock must distinguish available, reserved, in-treatment, quarantined and released quantities. Keep pack-to-batch and batch-to-recovered-stock lineage. Incoming packs begin available; quarantine prevents reservation or release. Only a quality officer can place or clear quarantine, with actor, time and reason. Clearing quarantine does not itself approve a batch or release recovered stock.

## Domestic batch planning
A planner can prepare versioned domestic batches naming a kiln, start time and whole input packs. A batch must contain 1–12 packs, all of the same species and thickness band. Do not split a pack between batches. Domestic treatment lasts exactly 36 hours, followed by four hours of kiln turnaround. Capacity is reserved for the half-open interval from start through start plus 40 hours; another batch may start exactly at that endpoint. Use UTC timestamps at whole-hour boundaries. A pack cannot belong to two outstanding approved batches, even on different kilns.

Drafts hold neither packs nor kiln time. A production supervisor can approve or cancel an unstarted batch. Approval atomically reserves both packs and kiln time, rejecting overlaps, unavailable packs and stale pack metadata. Concurrent approvals cannot reserve the same pack or overlapping time on one kiln. Cancellation releases both reservations and preserves history. Starting requires supervisor confirmation that the approved inputs remain valid and the kiln is not occupied; it moves the packs into treatment. An actual run that extends beyond its planned finish keeps the kiln occupied until actual completion plus four hours. Following approvals remain visible as conflicts and cannot start while the kiln is occupied; do not silently shift their start times.

## Completion and recovered stock
A completion record names the batch, actual start and finish, recovered board count and scrap board count. Finish cannot precede start, and recovered plus scrap must equal the input board count. Invalid completion records change nothing. Finishing treatment releases kiln occupancy after the four-hour turnaround, but recovered boards remain awaiting quality review. A quality officer can release recovered boards only after accepting the completion reconciliation and clearing every active quarantine affecting the batch. Scrap never becomes saleable stock. A revised completion must retain its earlier version and reason; if it changes a released board count, suspend the affected stock's release and require fresh quality review. Never create extra available input packs when recording recovered output.

## Export-treatment uncertainty
Export batches require a verified heat-treatment protocol in addition to ordinary capacity checks. The signed customer protocol says “the timber core must remain at or above 56°C for at least 30 consecutive minutes.” The plant's approved compliance manual says “the timber core must remain at or above 56°C for at least 60 consecutive minutes.” Both are mandatory and no precedence ruling is supplied. The plant compliance officer must resolve the contradiction. Do not approve export batches or request their certificates until a protocol is resolved and its required observations are defined. Do not infer a complete export cycle duration from either minimum hold time. Intake, domestic planning and domestic recovery review remain usable meanwhile. Users disagree whether the yard overview should initially group stock by supplier or species; either is acceptable and must not alter stock or capacity calculations.

## Certificate requests and authority
The external timber-certification service is expected to receive export-treatment evidence and return certificate-request status. Its endpoint, authentication, plant permissions, evidence schema, protocol identifiers, status meanings and duplicate-request handling are unknown. Live certificate exchange is blocked on those details and the export protocol, while local domestic work can use synthetic records.

A certificate request is an external attestation. Before any attempt, the plant compliance officer must separately authorize the exact export batch, completion revision and evidence version. This specification grants no execution permission. A later change to any authorized evidence or completion invalidates that authorization. Internal production approval and quality release are not permission to request a certificate. Preserve distinct outcomes of rejected, pending, certificate issued and unknown; pending is not issued. A timeout is unknown and another attempt requires documented outcome resolution rather than automatic retry. An issued certificate is linked to its authorized evidence and does not itself release stock.

Viewers can read; yard clerks can receive and amend intake; planners can prepare batches; production supervisors can approve, cancel and start them; quality officers manage quarantine, completion acceptance and stock release; only the plant compliance officer can resolve the export protocol and authorize certificate requests. Enforce permissions at the operation boundary, beyond hidden controls. The overview must show stock by state, kiln reservations and actual occupancy, schedule conflicts, recovery totals, quarantines and certificate status, with supporting record references.

## Acceptance and delivery expectations
A domestic batch starting 3 November 2026 at 08:00 UTC reserves its kiln until 5 November at 00:00 UTC. A second batch at that endpoint is allowed; one starting an hour earlier is rejected. Thirteen packs fail even when board counts are small. Two packs of different species fail even when slots remain. For 1,000 input boards, recovered 970 plus scrap 30 is valid; recovered 970 plus scrap 20 is rejected without changes. If actual treatment finishes on 5 November at 02:00 UTC, the kiln remains occupied until 06:00, and the approved 00:00 batch cannot start automatically. A certificate request marked pending leaves certificate-issued status false and does not release stock.

Records, lineage, authorization history and consistent stock and capacity results must survive restart. Local storage, receipt-preview layout and initial overview grouping may use explicitly labeled reversible technical assumptions; none is customer approval. Intake reconciliation and domestic kiln scheduling are independently plannable with synthetic data before export-policy or service details are resolved. Domestic recovery and quality review can also be planned locally. Describe checks as proposed verification: no implementation, executed tests or live integration evidence is supplied.
