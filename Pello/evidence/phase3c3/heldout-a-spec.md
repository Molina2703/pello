# CanalShare: irrigation allocation and delivery reconciliation

## Business purpose and boundaries
A water cooperative needs a planning service for allocating canal water among farm accounts and reconciling delivered volumes against seasonal entitlements. The pilot covers one canal, one April–September season and at most 200 accounts. All volumes are whole cubic metres (m³). Each operating day has 48 half-hour intervals in UTC; intervals include their start and exclude their end. The service does not control pumps or gates, establish water rights, bill customers, trade entitlements or optimize crop choices. Local synthetic data is available; live credentials, a service account and external-operation authorizations are absent.

## Entitlements and requests
A cooperative clerk records each account's stable identifier, seasonal entitlement and dated entitlement adjustments with reasons. Entitlements do not carry into the next season, and accounts cannot borrow from another account or a future season. Available allowance equals entitlement plus adjustments, minus finalized actual use, minus outstanding reservations. A negative allowance caused by actual over-delivery must remain visible; it permits no new reservation. Requests name an account, operating date, interval and positive whole-m³ quantity. There may be only one active request per account per interval. Changes to an entitlement, request or reservation must retain who changed it, when and why.

## Normal-day allocation
For each interval, gross deliverable water is the lesser of forecast supply and the canal limit of 120 m³. On a normal day, retain 20 m³ for the downstream environmental reserve; distributable water is max(0, gross deliverable minus reserve). Process intervals chronologically. For each interval, cap each request at that account's remaining allowance after earlier allocations. If capped demand fits, allocate it in full. Otherwise divide distributable water proportionally to capped demand, round down to whole m³ and distribute remaining units in descending fractional-remainder order, breaking ties by ascending account identifier. Never exceed a capped request. An unfilled request expires with its interval; do not move it to another interval automatically.

## Drought policy requiring resolution
The signed operating handbook states: “For every drought-designated interval, retain exactly 30 m³ as the environmental reserve.” The regulator's current instruction states: “For every drought-designated interval, retain exactly 40 m³ as the environmental reserve.” Both are described as mandatory, and no precedence decision has been supplied. Do not choose a value or offer a publishable drought allocation until the cooperative's water-rights officer resolves this conflict. Normal-day allocation, entitlement records and meter reconciliation must remain usable while this issue is open. Users also disagree whether the overview should group farms by village or turnout; either grouping is acceptable for the pilot, and it must not affect allocation.

## Schedule review and external submission
A planner can create a dated draft, inspect allocations and shortages, and create a new version after input changes. Drafts do not reserve entitlement. Internal approval reserves the approved quantities atomically; two approvals competing for the same allowance cannot both spend it. Approval must reject a draft whose entitlement, request or supply inputs have changed. Replacing an approved version must atomically replace its reservations, and cancelling it releases only its outstanding reservations. Reconciled usage remains charged. A viewer can read records; a clerk can maintain entitlements and reconcile meters; a planner can prepare schedules; only a water-rights officer can approve, replace or cancel schedules. Enforce these permissions beyond the screen controls.

The Canal Authority gateway is expected to provide supply forecasts and receive approved schedules. Its endpoint, authentication method, account permissions, payload schema, acknowledgement meaning and retry/idempotency behavior have not been provided. These are critical unknowns for live exchange, not reasons to delay local planning with explicitly labeled synthetic supply. Submission is an external commitment: a water-rights officer must separately authorize the exact canal, operating date and approved version before a submission attempt. Internal approval is not that authorization, and this specification grants no execution permission. Changed versions need fresh authorization. If an attempt's outcome is unknown, show it as unknown and require a documented resolution before another attempt; do not silently retry. Accepted, rejected and unknown outcomes must remain distinguishable.

## Meter reconciliation and visibility
Clerks can preview a local CSV containing reading_id, revision, account_id, interval_start_utc and delivered_m3. Each row is an interval total, not a cumulative counter. Quantities are nonnegative whole m³. Unknown accounts, off-grid interval starts and malformed rows reject the entire import with row-level reasons and no changes. The same reading_id and revision with the same content is a no-op; different content at that same revision rejects the import. A higher revision replaces the previous value and charges only the difference. A reading_id cannot change account or interval, and two reading_ids cannot represent the same account and interval. Lower revisions reject the import. Accepted totals finalize that interval's actual use, replacing any reservation for the same account and interval, including when delivered volume is zero. Actual use without a reservation is recorded and flagged. Corrections adjust finalized use without reviving reservations. Elapsed capacity is never reassigned. Users need an overview of entitlement, reserved volume, actual use, remaining allowance, shortages and unresolved delivery exceptions, with source/version information for forecasts and readings.

## Acceptance examples and delivery expectations
With normal supply of 120 m³, ample allowances and requests A=80 and B=40, allocations are A=67 and B=33. With equal requests A=1, B=1 and C=1 and normal supply of 22 m³, allocations are A=1, B=1 and C=0. With normal supply of 15 m³, all allocations are zero. An account with entitlement 150, finalized use 30 and outstanding reservations 40 has allowance 80. If its reserved interval of 40 is finalized at actual 35, its allowance becomes 85. Reimporting that reading changes nothing; a higher revision changing 35 to 38 makes allowance 82. No drought allocation result is asserted until the reserve conflict is resolved.

The pilot must preserve business records across restart and show clear validation and permission errors. Use reversible technical assumptions for local storage, CSV presentation and the optional overview grouping; identify these as assumptions rather than customer approvals. Entitlement accounting and CSV reconciliation can be planned and checked using local examples before gateway details or drought policy are resolved. Normal-day allocation can likewise be planned against synthetic supply. Describe future verification as proposed work; no implementation or test execution is supplied by this brief.
