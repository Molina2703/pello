# StackProof: industrial air-permit evidence and daily compliance

## Purpose and operating boundary
A ceramics manufacturer needs defensible daily emissions assessments and reviewed regulatory submissions. The pilot covers one plant, three stacks and two pollutants: particulate matter and nitrogen oxides (NOx). Permit days and hour boundaries use UTC. Concentrations arrive already normalized in mg/Nm³, with at most three decimal places; do not perform normalization conversions. The service does not control combustion equipment, tune instruments, predict dispersion, calculate carbon credits or replace emergency alarms. Synthetic measurements, operating logs and calibration records are available. External credentials, accounts and submission authorizations are absent.

## Monitoring register and operating evidence
An environmental technician maintains effective-dated assignments of instruments to stack and pollutant. Assignments for the same stack and pollutant cannot overlap. Preserve instrument identity and assignment history. Each stack-hour requires an operating status of OFF, NORMAL or STARTUP, supported by a source reference. A missing status is unknown, not OFF. Corrections to assignments or operating status retain actor, time, reason and previous values. OFF hours do not enter emissions averages, but their measurements remain in the evidence history.

Technicians can record instrument calibration intervals with start, end and reason. Intervals include their start and exclude their end; end must be later than start. Any overlap between a calibration interval and a measurement's five-minute interval invalidates that whole measurement for compliance, even if its source quality flag says valid. Calibration records and their corrections retain attribution and history. Register maintenance and calibration validation can be planned using local examples before a regulator connection exists.

## Measurement intake and corrections
A technician can preview a CSV containing delivery_id, measurement_id, instrument_id, interval_start_utc, concentration and source_quality. Each row represents exactly five minutes, beginning at a multiple of five minutes. Concentration must be nonnegative and source_quality must be VALID or INVALID. Reject the entire delivery, with row-level reasons and no changes, for malformed rows, unknown instruments, off-grid times, duplicate measurement identifiers or two measurements for the same instrument and interval. An identical delivery identifier and content is a no-op; changed content under that identifier is rejected. A later delivery cannot silently replace an existing measurement.

A quality reviewer can approve a correction naming the original measurement, replacement concentration or quality flag, reason and supporting reference. Preserve the raw value and every correction; only the latest approved correction is effective. Corrected data still obey calibration invalidation and instrument assignments. Measurement intake and correction review are independently plannable with synthetic files, without deciding disputed NOx policy.

## Particulate assessment
For each operating particulate stack-hour, expect 12 five-minute measurements from its assigned instrument. At least nine must be valid after source-quality, correction and calibration checks. Otherwise the hour is insufficient. An hour lacking an applicable instrument assignment is also insufficient. The hourly mean is the arithmetic mean of its valid concentrations. Compare exact calculated values with limits; round only displayed means to three decimal places, with exact halves rounded upward.

A particulate day is BREACH if any sufficient operating hour has a mean above 100 mg/Nm³, even when other hours are insufficient or have unknown status. Otherwise, if any stack-hour has unknown operating status or any operating hour is insufficient, the day is INDETERMINATE. Otherwise average all operating hourly means equally: a result above 50 mg/Nm³ is BREACH, and a result at or below 50 is COMPLIANT. A fully documented day with every hour OFF is NO OPERATION, not COMPLIANT and not a zero concentration. Assess each stack separately; do not average stacks together. STARTUP counts as operating for particulate assessment.

## NOx contradiction and review scope
The plant's signed NOx permit states “exclude STARTUP hours from the daily compliance assessment.” The regulator's current written direction states “include STARTUP hours in the daily compliance assessment.” Both are mandatory, with no precedence ruling supplied. The environmental compliance manager must resolve this contradiction. Do not issue an official NOx daily classification or submit NOx results before resolution and confirmation of the applicable NOx limits. Those limits have not been supplied. NOx intake, calibration checks and hourly evidence preparation can proceed; particulate assessment and particulate-only submission preparation must remain usable. Users also disagree whether the overview should initially group results by stack or date. Either grouping is acceptable and must not change classifications.

## Review, revisions and external filing
A reviewer can prepare a versioned daily report showing operating evidence, valid-measurement counts, hourly means, daily classification, exceptions and links to source records. Preparation does not authorize filing. Changes to relevant measurements, assignments, calibration or operating status mark an affected prepared report stale. Recalculation creates a new report version. A previously submitted version remains immutable and visible; corrections require a separately reviewed amendment linked to that submission. The overview must expose stale reports, insufficient hours, unknown statuses, breaches and filing outcomes rather than displaying missing results as zero.

The regulator portal is expected to accept daily reports and amendments and return filing outcomes. Its endpoint, authentication, plant permissions, payload schema, particulate-only filing support, acknowledgement meanings and duplicate-filing behavior are unknown. These block live filing, not local evidence preparation or particulate calculation. Before any attempt, the environmental compliance manager must separately authorize the exact report version, stack set, pollutant set and permit date. This brief grants no execution permission. Relevant evidence changes invalidate that authorization. Immediately before filing, reject stale or unauthorized versions rather than silently recalculating and sending a different report.

Preserve distinct filing outcomes of rejected, received for review, legally accepted and unknown. Receipt is not legal acceptance. A timeout is unknown; another attempt requires documented outcome resolution, with no automatic retry. Link every outcome to the attempted version and authorization. Viewers can read; technicians maintain registers, operating evidence, calibrations and intake; quality reviewers approve measurement corrections and prepare reports; only the environmental compliance manager resolves NOx policy and authorizes filings. Enforce permissions beyond screen visibility.

## Acceptance and delivery expectations
Nine valid particulate measurements of 40 and three invalid measurements produce a sufficient hourly mean of 40; eight valid measurements produce an insufficient hour. Ten operating hours averaging 40 and two averaging 70 give a daily mean of 45 and COMPLIANT when all other hours are documented OFF. A sufficient hour averaging 101 makes the day BREACH even with another hour insufficient. Twelve operating hours averaging exactly 50 give COMPLIANT. Calibration from 09:02 to 09:03 invalidates the 09:00–09:05 measurement. A day with all 24 statuses OFF yields NO OPERATION; one missing status prevents that result. No numerical NOx compliance outcome is asserted.

Evidence, corrections, report versions, authorizations and consistent classifications must survive restart. Local storage, CSV preview layout and initial grouping may use explicitly labeled reversible assumptions; these are not customer approvals. Describe verification as proposed work: no implementation, executed tests or live portal evidence is supplied.
