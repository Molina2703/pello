# StageMark: mountain-bike stage-race classification

## Purpose and limits
A race organizer needs defensible stage results and an overall classification for a four-stage mountain-bike event with up to 800 riders in two divisions. Riders compete individually; each has one immutable rider identifier, one unique bib and one division. Times use UTC and integer milliseconds. The service does not reserve starts, manage accommodation, collect fees, control timing hardware, provide emergency tracking or decide medical fitness. Synthetic entries, timing observations and jury decisions are available. External credentials, accounts and publication authorizations are absent.

## Entries and course evidence
An entries clerk can preview and accept a roster containing rider_id, bib, division and name. Missing fields, duplicate identifiers or bibs and unknown divisions reject the entire import with row-level reasons and no changes. Reimporting identical roster content is a no-op. Amendments retain actor, time, reason and previous values; changing a rider's division invalidates affected draft classifications. Preserve previous published results rather than rewriting them.

Each stage has an approved course version listing its start, ordered mandatory checkpoints, finish and common start timestamp. A timing clerk imports observations containing observation_id, rider_id, stage_id, control_id and observed_at. Unknown riders or controls and malformed timestamps reject the whole delivery. An identical observation identifier and content is a no-op; changed content under that identifier is rejected. Retain raw observations. The earliest observation at each control at or after the stage start is selected; later duplicates remain visible but do not add elapsed time. Pre-start observations are retained and ignored. A missing control or selected controls out of course order creates an unresolved timing exception, not an invented crossing or automatic disqualification.

A timing supervisor may approve a replacement selected crossing with reason and supporting evidence, preserving raw observations and correction history. A finish before the start is invalid. Entry validation and course/observation validation can be planned separately using synthetic data before external access or the disputed fourth-stage policy is resolved.

## Riding time and neutralization
For stages 1–3, gross time is selected finish minus common start. The jury can approve rider-specific neutralization intervals with evidence and reason. Intervals include their start and exclude their end, must end after they start and must lie within that rider's start-to-finish interval. Deduct the duration of their union, so overlapping intervals are never deducted twice. Net riding time is gross time minus that deduction. Invalid neutralization records change nothing. Net riding time at or below six hours meets the stage cutoff; time above six hours is OVER TIME LIMIT. Penalties do not affect the cutoff test.

A stage time penalty is a nonnegative integer number of milliseconds added to net riding time for stage ranking and overall totals. An overall-only penalty affects only the overall total and must not be applied once per stage. Every penalty names its rider, scope, jury decision reference and reason. Withdrawn penalties remain in history and cease contributing. A jury decision may explicitly disqualify a rider from a stage; timing exceptions alone cannot do so.

## Classification and a disputed stage
Rank eligible stage finishers within their division by net riding time plus stage penalties, lowest first. Exact ties share competition ranks, such as 1,1,3; never use bib order to break them. A rider qualifies for overall classification only after all four stages are resolved, within cutoff and without stage disqualification. Overall time sums the four ranked stage times and adds overall-only penalties once. Break an overall-time tie by lower fourth-stage ranked time; if still equal, share the rank.

The signed fourth-stage weather bulletin says “neutralized time is deducted when testing the six-hour cutoff.” The chief commissaire's signed direction says “neutralized time is not deducted when testing the six-hour cutoff.” Both are mandatory and no precedence ruling exists. The chief commissaire must resolve the contradiction. Deduction for fourth-stage scoring is agreed; only its cutoff treatment is disputed. Fourth-stage cutoff decisions and overall classification remain blocked. Entry work, timing reconciliation, penalties and final classifications for stages 1–3 must proceed. Do not infer cutoff policy from the more generous or more restrictive document. Users disagree whether review screens should initially group exceptions by rider or control; either is acceptable and must not change results.

## Jury review and result versions
A jury secretary records appeals against a crossing, neutralization, penalty or disqualification, with rider, stage or overall scope, reason and evidence reference. An open appeal blocks publication only of the classifications it could affect. The jury chair records an upheld or rejected decision with reasons; upholding an appeal requires an explicit linked correction or decision, not a silent change to a total. An appeal about an overall-only penalty does not block an unaffected stage classification.

A results officer can prepare versioned classifications exposing selected crossings, exceptions, neutralized duration, penalties, cutoff status, ranks and source references. Relevant entry, course, timing or jury changes make affected drafts stale. Approval rejects stale or unresolved classifications. Published versions remain immutable; changes require a newly approved amendment linked to the prior publication. Review screens must expose unresolved timing, open appeals, stale drafts and publication outcomes.

## External publication and authority
The federation service is expected to receive official classifications and amendments and return publication outcomes. Its endpoint, authentication, event permissions, division codes, payload schema, acknowledgement meanings and duplicate-publication behavior are unknown. These block live publication, not local calculation. Before any attempt, the race director must separately authorize the exact approved result version, event, stage or overall scope and divisions. This brief grants no execution permission. Changed results invalidate that authorization; results approval is not publication permission. Recheck currency, unresolved appeals and authorization immediately before sending.

Preserve rejected, received for processing, published and unknown outcomes. Receipt is not publication. A timeout is unknown; another attempt requires documented outcome resolution rather than automatic retry. Link outcomes to the attempted version and authorization. Entries clerks maintain entries; timing clerks import observations; timing supervisors approve crossing corrections; jury secretaries record appeals; jury chairs approve neutralizations, penalties, disqualifications and appeal decisions; results officers prepare and approve classifications; only the race director authorizes publication. Viewers can read approved classifications. Enforce permissions beyond screen visibility. The chief commissaire alone resolves the disputed cutoff policy.

## Acceptance and delivery expectations
A gross time of 5h50m with neutralizations 02:00–02:20 and 02:10–02:30 after the start has a 30-minute union and net time 5h20m. Adding a ten-minute stage penalty gives ranked time 5h30m. A net time of exactly six hours meets the cutoff even with a penalty; six hours plus one millisecond does not. Stage times totaling 20 hours plus an overall-only five-minute penalty produce 20h05m. Equal ranked times followed by a slower rider receive ranks 1,1,3. A missing checkpoint remains unresolved. No fourth-stage cutoff or overall winner is asserted before policy resolution.

Evidence, jury history, result versions and authorizations must survive restart with identical times and ranks. Local storage, preview layout and exception grouping may use explicitly labeled reversible assumptions; none is customer approval. Verification is proposed work: no implementation, executed tests or live federation evidence is supplied.
